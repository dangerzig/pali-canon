PREFACE. 


In preparing the present edition of the Udåna I have 
made use of the following MSS. :— 

. A. MS. of the India Office, Burmese writing. 

B. MS. presented to the Pali Text Society by the Thera 
Süriyagoda Sonuttara in Kandy, Sinhalese writing. 

D. Copy made for Professor Rhys Davids at Kaluttara, 
Ceylon, in 1885. Sinhalese characters. The first MS. was 
kindly lent to me by Dr. Rost, the two latter MSS. by 
Professor Rhys Davids, who was also good enough to let me 
have the use of a MS. of a commentary! entitled the 
Paramattha-dipant, copied, 1884, in Ceylon, in Sinhalese 
writing, for the Páli Text Society through the kind inter- 
vention of Gooneratne Mudaliyar. This latter, which I 
quote as C, could only occasionally be employed for the 
restoration of the text, many passages being only paraphrased, 
not cited. There is no doubt but that the Commentator 
quotes several various readings not existing in our MSS. 

Of these MSS. of the text, the most reliable is A, although 
some few errors in spelling, bb for pp, ño for ñ], are found 
in it, and we (cannot depend upon it in the matter of the 
use of Anusvara, and of the marks of quantity. D, a less 
correct MS. than A, but a better one than B, has several 
blunders common with A, from which it may be concluded 
that the two MSS. have been derived from the same original.

The Udåna, a small collection of Buddhist stories and 
sentences, is divided into eight Vaggas, each of which con- 
tains ten Suttas, and is named after one of them. Several 
Suttas are found in the Mahåvagga, the Cullavagga and 
the Mahåparinibbåna Sutta. Each sutta is concluded by 
an Udåna of Buddha, composed, for the most part, in ordinary 
metres (Cloka, Tristubh or Jagati), seldom in prose (comp. 
III. 10, VIII. 1. 3. 4).
